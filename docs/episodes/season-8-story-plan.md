# Lark & Nib’s Treasure Quest

## Season 8: The Atlas We Make

Writing plan · October 30, 2026 · 8 chapters · Chosen reading, strategic help, evidence-based explanation, and independent-purpose use

Planned complete-episode target: **15–17 minutes**, including normal child responses. This is a conditional writing allowance, not measured runtime or a reason to extend a tired child’s session. Use the shorter earlier-season target whenever engagement calls for it.

This is a mini-novel plan and backward-design reference. The [episode briefs](season-8-episode-briefs.md) provide the individual scene and interaction plans. The [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) holds cross-season rules and the cumulative teaching ledger. These replace older prototype story placeholders for writing; they do not change the deployed games.

## The starting state

Seven local adventures have ended with useful books and working places. The library atlas has a blank page for one new route selected by the child: the sunny meadow, pine lookout, or reed-bank reading place. The crew has its ordinary kit and portable story press. The child chooses a purpose and leads; Lark and Nib remain capable companions, not disappearing teachers.

## Plan the ending first

The child leads the selected outing using a route page built from actual read information and observations. The page states where to go, what to bring, where to stop, and a relevant alternative if conditions change. The crew returns, prints and binds The Atlas We Make, and leaves a usable atlas page for another reader. An explorer badge marks participation in a real shared project, not certified reading mastery. All three route endings close fully, and no path is framed as the best or most advanced choice.

## The mini novel

The final mini novel belongs to the child’s choices. A meadow page promises a pleasant sunny stop, a pine page a broad sheltered view, and a reed page a quiet waterside reading place. Whichever is chosen, the first plan is incomplete: a second source adds a useful preparation, an unfamiliar word may invite help, and a later topic adds a condition the first cheerful page omitted. The child tells the crew what the sources actually say, compares the related information, and builds a route that serves its purpose rather than merely following the first attractive picture. A fresh page then supplies one final condition to interpret without rehearsal. In the last chapter, Lark follows the child’s route and Nib carries out an assigned practical job. The completed atlas page is the concrete result of choosing, reading, asking, explaining, and checking. This concludes the series’s movement from following one little sound to making information useful for somebody else.

## Work backward from that ending

1. The final outing needs four usable pieces of route information: destination, equipment, stopping place, and a condition/alternative. Collect them in separate earlier chapters.
2. The printed page needs an authored summary and verified observations; the child cannot receive a prewritten perfect atlas as a reward.
3. The fresh page needs already-taught code and a topic the child understands; remove hidden new spellings before its first presentation.
4. Optional help must support a real uncertainty, not become a required Ask Joyce ritual.
5. Every branch must supply comparable decisions and a complete ending; do not make one route secretly safer, easier, or more valuable as a reading grade.

## Character movement

Lark becomes a partner who trusts the child’s stated reason while asking useful questions. Nib becomes a helper who can wait, carry, and check rather than supply every comic wrong answer. The child chooses an interest and purpose, changes a plan when evidence calls for it, and leads with support available instead of performing independence for a reward.

## Object, information, and destination ledger

- The branch ledger records the selected route and its four information slots. Untaken routes are not described as visited.
- Common episode functions are shared, but destination details, equipment, weather condition, clue page, and final illustrated atlas entry differ by branch.
- The portable press from Season 6 prints from the actual child-approved source with visible supplies; the field lens and earlier books remain ordinary tools.
- Selected route labels are sun, pine, and reed, all prepared spellings. Meadow, ridge, and field are narrator-supplied names unless their additional spellings are explicitly taught.

## Earlier setups and later payoffs

- The first personal purpose is checked against the final outing, rather than replaced by a test score.
- A second source’s condition revises the first attractive plan.
- The child’s own explanation from 4 becomes the draft revised in 6 and printed in 8.
- The crew roles reverse at the ending: Lark follows the route, Nib performs the child-assigned practical task.

## Teaching and review handoff

Every chapter carries the entire earlier taught set forward. Its listed review is a selection, not a reset. New sound/spelling models precede child use. Words can be read as soon as their mappings are introduced; no later “blending” chapter postpones that work. The cumulative ledger in the series roadmap records the exact introduction points for patterns and explicit function words.

The example lists are deliberately limited child-reading targets. A character name, chapter title, spoken sound-bank word, or narrated instruction is not automatically decodable. Narrator-supplied names and untaught text stay supplied. Capital forms are paired explicitly with familiar lowercase forms before they are required. Actual responses guide support; completion is not mastery.

For each new-code chapter, model the focus and use its sound bank disproportionately in natural prose. For comprehension chapters, state the text strategy and continue selective decoding review. Use expressive speech attributions frequently; audition the intended delivery in the recording. Check the entire drafted child-readable passage against the ledger, not only these examples.

## Physical staging and production

Prepare three coherent route sets: broad dry meadow path with a shaded stop; pine path with a level viewing platform and shelter; reed-bank path with a dry boardwalk and fixed reading platform. Never put a character’s feet on water to make the creek branch fit a recycled plate. Source pages and final page images must show the selected route’s actual geometry.

Before generating a plate, specify foot/prop contact planes, attachment points, walking corridors, and moving-platform parents. Composite the real layers at tablet crops. Mouth-only speech and body-only walking remain the motion style. Plan scenes around actual supporting geometry; do not reuse an attractive river plate by placing Nib on its water.

Choose a story-audio budget from the total target after reserving ordinary response and transition time. Measure an expressive read-through, then the recording and played chapter. Never insert extra practice or hurry speech just to meet a duration. New audio requires its own alignment and cuts. Scripts, art, recordings, and builds remain separate production work.

## Resolution and next-season invitation

Each branch returns to camp with an accurate atlas entry and a resolved outing. Book eight joins the earlier seven. The child can reread, choose another branch later, or select a new book; there is no new locked mystery required to finish this series.

## Writing acceptance

Before a script is recorded, verify that each opening follows the previous end state, each chapter resolves its own question, and each destination follows from something actually observed or read. Check the ledger, review opportunities, independent/support distinctions, and setup/payoff chain. No unprepared word, new magical power, or hidden assessment threshold may appear only at the finale.
