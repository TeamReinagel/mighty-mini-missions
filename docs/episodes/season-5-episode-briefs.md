# The Clockwork Garden: Episode Briefs

Season 5 · 16 individual story plans · October 30, 2026

Read the [season arc](season-5-story-plan.md) first and use the [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) for earlier teaching. These are script-ready planning beats, not finished prose or recorded episodes. All chapters target 12–14 minutes for the whole played experience, conditionally on engagement.

## Shared writing and response conventions

Write narrated connective prose between attributed dialogue. Each outline below gives a setup, complication, meaningful child contribution, and resolved consequence. Expand those beats into a story rather than reading the outline aloud. Keep performance directions out of the spoken manuscript. Speaker names immediately after quotations preserve compatibility with the current attribution parser.

R = review before a hint; M = modeled/guided teaching; A = application before answer-revealing help; S = ungraded story action. A newly demonstrated answer is supported practice, not independent decoding. A word-label choice shows recognition; a tap does not prove oral reading. Speech stays optional and has a touch-supported continuation. Never invent a response the child did not give.

Each chapter has a short review, a model of its focus, and three story applications below. Combine adjacent actions at natural gates when that improves pacing; the five rows are a writing plan, not a compulsory minimum test count. Record first responses only where they change the next moment. Help responds to the actual choice and keeps the next attempt available. Playback cuts after the prompt and before its outcome.

## Episode 1 · Sunset at the Wrong Time

**Story question:** Why does the garden’s noon chime sound near sunset?

**Opening state:** The Storyboat has completed its first circuit. A garden keeper borrows an illustrated maintenance guide and asks why the closed garden’s little clock and watering wheel no longer agree. The crew arrives as helpers with a real guide, not as owners of a new magical mechanism. The library, post, lookout, and Storyboat remain operating elsewhere.

**New teaching / next step:** split familiar closed chunks in a compound; keep the full word’s meaning

**Selected review:** sun, set, boat, book

**Child-reading targets:** **sunset**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** sunset, sun, set, garden, clock, stopped

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `problem-solving-4`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. The keeper demonstrates a chime that disagrees with the garden’s visible cycle. Nib proposes a larger bell.

2. Lark models sun/set as readable familiar chunks and joins sunset, connecting the word to the actual scene.

3. The child marks the observation in the guide’s blank note without pretending it already explains the cause.

4. They keep the mismatch as a question and begin making a usable inspection place for the next morning.
