The Yes That Meant Yet
Lark & Nib's Treasure Quest · Season 3, Episode 5

Nib saw the first letters of a stopping note and brightened. The van waited behind him on broad ground. Ahead, one fastening on the crossing support was still loose. Lark put a wing beside the very last letter before Nib could call the route ready.

“Yes!” Nib exclaimed. “Yet,” Lark corrected gently, pointing to the very last letter.

Lark opened an earlier journal page on a flat, dry surface. Nib steadied its lower corner. One familiar word waited for another look.

“Try our earlier word before we start,” Lark invited gently. “You can sound it out or ask for help.”

> cape

“At the beginning of yes and yet, y has its consonant sound,” Lark modeled clearly. “Join it to e and s for yes. Now change only the last sound to t: yet. The beginning doesn't tell us enough to choose between them.”

“Join the parts and check the whole meaning,” Lark invited gently. “You can hear a part again whenever you want.”

On a little practice strip, you could try yes, yet, yap. Lark supplied unfamiliar story names herself. Nib waited while you looked right through each word.

“Read the final word on our stopping note,” Lark invited gently. “Does it say yes, or yet?”

A short passage stayed in view. Lark paired each needed capital with its lowercase form and familiar sound before you began, leaving the sentence meanings for you to discover.

“Read the sentences in order,” Lark invited gently. “Let your finger follow if it helps. When you are ready, use the words to answer our question.”

> Sam can yap. Pat can not nap yet.

“Who cannot nap yet?” Lark asked gently. “What is Sam doing in the other sentence?”

Lark kept the passage open so you could point to the part that helped. Nib listened before adding the picture.

The longer message, supplied by Lark, was not yet. Nib stayed behind the support while its visible fastening was secured. Lark checked the supported boards, and the van crossed slowly only when the load path was ready. On the far side, a duck greeted you beside the next marker with an enthusiastic quack.

“Let's keep a written clue,” Lark suggested warmly. “Write y beside our picture. You can look at the example, follow it first, or try your own version. Then reread what you made.”

Your journal kept the two ending letters side by side. The load was safely across, and waiting had finished its useful job. Nib listened to the duck's greeting without pretending it was permission for the next turn.
