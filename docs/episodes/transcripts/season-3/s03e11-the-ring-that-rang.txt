The Ring That Rang
Lark & Nib's Treasure Quest · Season 3, Episode 11

The connecting ring made a pleasant little clang. Nib tested it again, enjoying the note until the shutter stopped halfway open. Lark showed him where the retaining pin had slipped from its proper seat. The music had come from a joint that needed fastening.

“A ring that rings,” Nib observed delightedly. “A ring that stays on,” Lark reminded him warmly.

Lark opened an earlier journal page on a flat, dry surface. Nib steadied its lower corner. One familiar word waited for another look.

“Try our earlier word before we start,” Lark invited gently. “You can sound it out or ask for help.”

“In ring and sing, ng writes the single sound /ng/,” Lark modeled clearly. “Keep the ending smooth. Don't add a separate hard g after it. Compare pin and ring, listening to how their endings differ.”

“Join the parts and check the whole meaning,” Lark invited gently. “You can hear a part again whenever you want.”

On a little practice strip, you could try ring, sing, long, king. Lark supplied unfamiliar story names herself. Nib waited while you looked right through each word.

“Read ring on the joint note,” Lark invited gently. “Which fastening belongs to that ring?”

A short passage stayed in view. Lark paired each needed capital with its lowercase form and familiar sound before you began, leaving the sentence meanings for you to discover.

“Read the sentences in order,” Lark invited gently. “Let your finger follow if it helps. When you are ready, use the words to answer our question.”

“Who has a ring?” Lark asked softly. “Does the passage say that both people have one?”

Lark kept the passage open so you could point to the part that helped. Nib listened before adding the picture.

Nib held the unloaded ring still while Lark seated the retaining pin. They tested one small shutter movement. The ring stayed connected, and the shutter completed its travel without a clang. The caretaker brought the next fitting: a long tube meant to carry an ordinary signal chime toward the meeting place.

“Let's keep a written clue,” Lark suggested warmly. “Write ring beside our picture. You can look at the example, follow it first, or try your own version. Then reread what you made.”

Lark drew the secured joint. Nib listened to its new silence with approval. The shutter worked; the chime still needed a tube that pointed toward the people who were meant to hear it.
