The Rope at the Hill Home
Lark & Nib's Treasure Quest · Season 3, Episode 7

At the hill lodging, Nib looped a rope around the cloth cape. Lark stopped the lift before the cloth took the weight. The case rested securely below the fixed frame. Its real load points were visible beneath the handle, exactly where the guide's sling diagram showed them.

“A strong cape,” Nib suggested hopefully. “A stronger place to hold it,” Lark urged calmly.

Lark opened an earlier journal page on a flat, dry surface. Nib steadied its lower corner. One familiar word waited for another look.

“Try our earlier word before we start,” Lark invited gently. “You can sound it out or ask for help.”

> quit

“In rope and note, o and final silent e give the vowel its long sound,” Lark modeled clearly. “Compare hop with hope, then read rope. We don't use a long o every time we see o; the spelling of the whole word helps us choose.”

“Join the parts and check the whole meaning,” Lark invited gently. “You can hear a part again whenever you want.”

On a little practice strip, you could try rope, home, note. Lark supplied unfamiliar story names herself. Nib waited while you looked right through each word.

“Read rope on the proper sling's label,” Lark invited gently. “Which attachment points does the guide give it?”

A short passage stayed in view. Lark paired each needed capital with its lowercase form and familiar sound before you began, leaving the sentence meanings for you to discover.

“Read the sentences in order,” Lark invited gently. “Let your finger follow if it helps. When you are ready, use the words to answer our question.”

> Sam can get a rope. Pat can make a note.

“Who can record something in writing?” Lark asked gently. “Which word tells you?”

Lark kept the passage open so you could point to the part that helped. Nib listened before adding the picture.

Nib moved the loop from the cover to the case's load points. They raised it a small distance, checked the sling, and lowered it once before the full lift. The frame brought the glass onto the fixed platform intact. The cape stayed a cover. Unfortunately, the packing box's opening now faced the platform rail.

“Let's keep a written clue,” Lark suggested warmly. “Write rope beside our picture. You can look at the example, follow it first, or try your own version. Then reread what you made.”

The journal showed the sling beneath the case rather than squeezing its cape. Lark parked the lifting gear. The glass had reached its destination safely; opening the supported box would take one more careful look.
