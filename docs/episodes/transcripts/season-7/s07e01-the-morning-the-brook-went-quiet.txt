The Morning the Brook Went Quiet
Lark & Nib's Treasure Quest · Season 7, Episode 1

The story press worked, and the garden remained open. On the familiar path, Nib stopped to listen. The brook sounded unusually quiet, and less water showed between its usual stones. Lark brought the field notebook, the ordinary viewing lens, and a borrowed channel diagram. The moonstone stayed at camp; this question needed observations.

“A brook having a nap?” Nib wondered softly. “A brook we should look at carefully,” Lark replied.

Lark opened an earlier journal page on a flat, dry surface. Nib steadied its lower corner. One familiar word waited for another look.

“Try our earlier word before we start,” Lark invited gently. “You can sound it out or ask for help.”

“A useful observation note gives a place, a time, and what we actually notice,” Lark explained gently. “I can supply the word quiet; it isn't an independent spelling task today. Read note and brook, then keep what we see separate from our ideas about why.”

“Join the parts and check the whole meaning,” Lark invited gently. “You can hear a part again whenever you want.”

On a little practice strip, you could try note, brook. Lark supplied unfamiliar story names herself. Nib waited while you looked right through each word.

“Which picture records the change beside this same path?” Lark invited gently. “What can we record before choosing an explanation?”

A short passage stayed in view. Lark paired each needed capital with its lowercase form and familiar sound before you began, leaving the sentence meanings for you to discover.

“Read the sentences in order,” Lark invited gently. “Let your finger follow if it helps. When you are ready, use the words to answer our question.”

“What does the passage report about the brook?” Lark asked gently. “Does it tell us why?”

Lark kept the passage open so you could point to the part that helped. Nib listened before adding the picture.

Lark dated the notebook's new observation. Nib drew the same stones with the lower visible water, leaving space below for questions. A brook taking a nap was an amusing idea, not a measured cause. The diagram offered a way to inspect the upstream route without deciding the answer in advance.

“Let's keep a written clue,” Lark suggested warmly. “Write note beside our picture. You can look at the example, follow it first, or try your own version. Then reread what you made.”

Your first investigation page kept the question open. The crew packed the notebook and followed the dry path toward the fixed bridge, ready to use the diagram's arrows.
