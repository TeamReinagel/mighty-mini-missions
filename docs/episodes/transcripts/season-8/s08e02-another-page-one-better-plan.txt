Lark & Nib's Treasure Quest · Season 8, Episode 2

## Sun route: meadow

The meadow invitation promised space to enjoy a picnic or look at flowers. Its second page showed a shade cloth and a mat. Nib brought a whole tower of workshop boxes. Lark put the equipment page beside the four planning spaces and waited to see what the outing actually required.

“The entire workshop?” Nib offered hopefully. “The things our page asks for,” Lark suggested gently.

Lark opened an earlier journal page on a flat, dry surface. Nib steadied its lower corner. One familiar word waited for another look.

“Try our earlier word before we start,” Lark invited gently. “You can sound it out or ask for help.”

“A second source can add something the first leaves out,” Lark explained gently. “Read its preparation words, then connect them to your chosen purpose. We need a reason for each item, not every item that happens to fit in a bag.”

“Join the parts and check the whole meaning,” Lark invited gently. “You can hear a part again whenever you want.”

On a little practice strip, you could try pack, kit, shade, coat. Lark supplied unfamiliar story names herself. Nib waited while you looked right through each word.

“Read the meadow equipment passage,” Lark invited gently. “Which two things serve this outing?”

“Read the sentences in order,” Lark invited gently. “Let your finger follow if it helps. When you are ready, use the words to answer our question.”

> Pack the mat. Pack the shade cloth.

“Which two things does the page ask us to pack?” Lark asked gently.

Lark kept the passage open so you could point to the part that helped. Nib listened before adding the picture.

Nib returned the workshop boxes. The shade cloth and mat went into the kit, with their attachment pieces packed beside them. Lark left the destination in its own space and added the actual equipment to the next. The page had improved the same meadow plan rather than sending the crew on a different outing.

“Let's keep a written clue,” Lark suggested warmly. “Write mat beside our picture. You can look at the example, follow it first, or try your own version. Then reread what you made.”

Your meadow draft now named its useful kit. If a word or instruction still needed help, the same page would remain available before anyone had to explain the plan.
