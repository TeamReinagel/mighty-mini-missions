Go to the Little Shelter
Lark & Nib's Treasure Quest · Season 5, Episode 2

Morning showed two shelters. Nib chose the tiny roof behind a root because it looked cozy. The work table would not fit beneath it. Lark measured its footprint against the clear shelter beside the working path. The useful place was not the smallest or the prettiest.

“A cozy little shelter,” Nib declared. “A table-sized shelter,” Lark suggested gently.

Lark opened an earlier journal page on a flat, dry surface. Nib steadied its lower corner. One familiar word waited for another look.

“Try our earlier word before we start,” Lark invited gently. “You can sound it out or ask for help.”

> sunset

“A syllable ending in a vowel can have a long vowel,” Lark modeled clearly. “Listen to me with long e, go with long o, and hi with long i. Robot begins with long ro, and unit begins with /yoo/. Baby begins with long ba; its final y says long e, unlike y in my. Read those modeled chunks and rejoin each word. These examples are useful, with exceptions still possible.”

“Join the parts and check the whole meaning,” Lark invited gently. “You can hear a part again whenever you want.”

On a little practice strip, you could try me, go, hi, robot, baby, unit. Lark supplied unfamiliar story names herself. Nib waited while you looked right through each word.

“Read go in the supplied route note,” Lark invited gently. “Which shelter leaves room for the actual table?”

A short passage stayed in view. Lark paired each needed capital with its lowercase form and familiar sound before you began, leaving the sentence meanings for you to discover.

“Read the sentences in order,” Lark invited gently. “Let your finger follow if it helps. When you are ready, use the words to answer our question.”

> Go to the shelter. The baby can nap.

“Where does the first sentence send us?” Lark asked gently. “Does the second sentence give another direction or tell what someone can do?”

Lark kept the passage open so you could point to the part that helped. Nib listened before adding the picture.

Nib carried the light kit to the clear shelter. The keeper brought the table, and the closed guide rested fully on top. The crew could work beside the path without blocking it. The smaller roof remained a perfectly pleasant place for something smaller than a work station.

“Let's keep a written clue,” Lark suggested warmly. “Write go beside our picture. You can look at the example, follow it first, or try your own version. Then reread what you made.”

Lark drew the real table footprint in your journal. The guide was dry inside the chosen shelter. Its cover showed beautiful plants, but the next useful page would be the one about the working mechanism.
