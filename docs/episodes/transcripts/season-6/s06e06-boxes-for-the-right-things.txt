Boxes for the Right Things
Lark & Nib's Treasure Quest · Season 6, Episode 6

The workshop had several bins, but every label named only one object. Nib put everything that looked numerous into the biggest box. When Lark asked for a clean sheet, a handle arrived first. The station needed labels matched to contents rather than container size.

“A box for boxes,” Nib proposed enthusiastically. “A label for the things inside,” Lark replied kindly.

Lark opened an earlier journal page on a flat, dry surface. Nib steadied its lower corner. One familiar word waited for another look.

“Try our earlier word before we start,” Lark invited gently. “You can sound it out or ask for help.”

> endless

“Plural endings can have different sounds,” Lark modeled clearly. “Cats ends with /s/, bins with /z/, and boxes with an extra /iz/ syllable. Read the base and ending, then connect the word to more than one of the things it names.”

“Join the parts and check the whole meaning,” Lark invited gently. “You can hear a part again whenever you want.”

On a little practice strip, you could try cats, bins, boxes. Lark supplied unfamiliar story names herself. Nib waited while you looked right through each word.

“Read the storage labels,” Lark invited gently. “Which one belongs to the clean sheets, and which names the tools?”

A short passage stayed in view. Lark paired each needed capital with its lowercase form and familiar sound before you began, leaving the sentence meanings for you to discover.

“Read the sentences in order,” Lark invited gently. “Let your finger follow if it helps. When you are ready, use the words to answer our question.”

> The cats sit by the bins. I can shut the boxes.

“What can the speaker shut?” Lark asked gently. “Which sentence names the cats?”

Lark kept the passage open so you could point to the part that helped. Nib listened before adding the picture.

Nib sorted the supplies into their intended bins. Lark attached the corrected labels, and they tried a real retrieval. A sheet came from the paper place, and a tool came from its own box. Beside them, a painted cover had finished drying over several story days. An older note still described it as waiting.

“Let's keep a written clue,” Lark suggested warmly. “Write boxes beside our picture. You can look at the example, follow it first, or try your own version. Then reread what you made.”

Your journal recorded the working storage layout. The next helper could find paper without opening every box. The finished cover needed its old progress note brought up to date.
