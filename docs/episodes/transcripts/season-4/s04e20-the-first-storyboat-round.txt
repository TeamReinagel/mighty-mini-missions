The First Storyboat Round
Lark & Nib's Treasure Quest · Season 4, Episode 20

Four flags waited beside the berth. Nib wanted to raise them together. Lark laid out the lending record first: Sea at Commons, Moon at Reed Creek, Train at Farm Quay, and Phone at Mill Pond. Each flag needed a real completed loan behind it.

“Four flags,” Nib declared proudly. “Four readers with their books,” Lark added with satisfaction.

Lark opened an earlier journal page on a flat, dry surface. Nib steadied its lower corner. One familiar word waited for another look.

“Try our earlier word before we start,” Lark invited gently. “You can sound it out or ask for help.”

> phone

“We can use the spellings we have already met,” Lark reminded you warmly. “Read boat and book, listening to their different vowels. Read train and phone with their prepared letter teams. Then check what each title means in our actual lending record.”

“Join the parts and check the whole meaning,” Lark invited gently. “You can hear a part again whenever you want.”

On a little practice strip, you could try boat, book, train, phone. Lark supplied unfamiliar story names herself. Nib waited while you looked right through each word.

“Match the four slips to the four destination pictures,” Lark invited gently. “Which title is written on each current loan?”

A short passage stayed in view. Lark paired each needed capital with its lowercase form and familiar sound before you began, leaving the sentence meanings for you to discover.

“Read the sentences in order,” Lark invited gently. “Let your finger follow if it helps. When you are ready, use the words to answer our question.”

> The boat has a book. The team can read it.

“What can the team read?” Lark asked gently. “Which word does it refer back to?”

Lark kept the passage open so you could point to the part that helped. Nib listened before adding the picture.

The slips matched the four stops. Nib raised four flags. Visitors joined a shared shore reading while the lent books remained with their borrowers. A brass boat badge joined the collection. Lark bound your twenty chapters into The Storyboat, a record of the lending service you had helped make usable.

“Let's keep a written clue,” Lark suggested warmly. “Write boat beside our picture. You can look at the example, follow it first, or try your own version. Then reread what you made.”

The fourth book joined the library. After the boat round was fully finished, a garden keeper brought a separate request for an illustrated maintenance guide. The flags marked completed loans, not promises of jobs still left undone.
