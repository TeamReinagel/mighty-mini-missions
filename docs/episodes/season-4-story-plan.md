# Lark & Nib’s Treasure Quest

## Season 4: The Storyboat

Writing plan · October 30, 2026 · 20 chapters · Vowel/consonant teams, r-controlled vowels, and selected alternative spellings

Planned complete-episode target: **11–13 minutes**, including normal child responses. This is a conditional writing allowance, not measured runtime or a reason to extend a tired child’s session. Use the shorter earlier-season target whenever engagement calls for it.

This is a mini-novel plan and backward-design reference. The [episode briefs](season-4-episode-briefs.md) provide the individual scene and interaction plans. The [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) holds cross-season rules and the cumulative teaching ledger. These replace older prototype story placeholders for writing; they do not change the deployed games.

## The starting state

The library, post circuit, and lookout signal work. A riverside invitation asks the crew to operate a small traveling library boat for four agreed stops. The existing ferry service is not abandoned: a separate broad, moored bookboat is available, with its own caretaker and fixed landing places. Four requested books travel in four dry compartments.

## Plan the ending first

The Storyboat completes its first full four-stop lending circuit. Sea, Moon, Train, and Phone have reached their agreed borrowers, the lending record is accurate, and the boat returns with its crew and remaining books dry. A little brass boat badge and the fourth mini novel commemorate a service that now belongs to the riverside community. The garden’s request for a practical guide opens a separate next adventure.

## The mini novel

A larger crew and a moving library make Lark’s tidy labels more complicated. The first landing needs a usable seat, chest, ramp, and lending record, not just a boat pointed in the right direction. Across the next three stops, a snagged mooring, muddled reading place, different useful spellings, and unclear instructions test whether the team can make reading work for its actual borrowers. Nib’s inventions help when they fit the setting; Lark learns to listen to people’s needs instead of arranging everyone around her diagram. By the return leg, the crew knows which book is lent, where it went, and how each landing can be used again. The climax is a complete first service and a shared reading on shore, rather than a dangerous storm or a stolen-book chase.

## Work backward from that ending

1. The final circuit needs four fulfilled requests and a correct lending record; introduce all four at the opening and mark each once.
2. The last landing needs a readable contact note and a real reachable shelf; wh/ph chapters prepare its labels and ordinary speaking tube.
3. Farm Quay needs its requested Train book and stable access; long-a spellings precede reading that title.
4. Reed Creek needs Moon plus a usable book rest; teach the two oo sounds separately before the book-rest chapter.
5. The first Commons stop needs a seat, chest, ramp, and record; ea/ch/ar/er establish these early jobs.

## Character movement

Lark moves from arranging a perfect service on paper to adapting it to real readers. Nib becomes a considerate collaborator who asks where an invention will be used. The child learns that a familiar sound can have another spelling, and that the same letters can sometimes require a different taught pronunciation.

## Object, information, and destination ledger

- B1 Sea is delivered in 4, B2 Moon in 9, B3 Train in 15, B4 Phone in 19; these four names are narrator-supplied until their spellings are taught.
- The four compartments are dry and visibly labeled; borrowing is recorded, not treated as a permanently lost book. Books are not required to be returned before their agreed lending period ends.
- A fixed gangplank, mooring rope, chest, seat, and shelf support actual objects; the boat badge is a finale collectible, not a key that unlocks every dock.
- The moonstone stays at camp and gains no power over water or machinery.

## Earlier setups and later payoffs

- The opening’s four requests become the finale’s four confirmed ledger entries.
- The awkward seat is refitted for its actual borrower instead of discarded.
- The two oo pronunciations explain why a familiar-looking spelling sometimes needs a new model.
- The rough first lending note becomes a clear usable record when all four stops are complete.

## Teaching and review handoff

Every chapter carries the entire earlier taught set forward. Its listed review is a selection, not a reset. New sound/spelling models precede child use. Words can be read as soon as their mappings are introduced; no later “blending” chapter postpones that work. The cumulative ledger in the series roadmap records the exact introduction points for patterns and explicit function words.

The example lists are deliberately limited child-reading targets. A character name, chapter title, spoken sound-bank word, or narrated instruction is not automatically decodable. Narrator-supplied names and untaught text stay supplied. Capital forms are paired explicitly with familiar lowercase forms before they are required. Actual responses guide support; completion is not mastery.

For each new-code chapter, model the focus and use its sound bank disproportionately in natural prose. For comprehension chapters, state the text strategy and continue selective decoding review. Use expressive speech attributions frequently; audition the intended delivery in the recording. Check the entire drafted child-readable passage against the ledger, not only these examples.

## Physical staging and production

New plates: a broad bookboat deck, Commons landing, Reed Creek reading bank, Farm Quay, Mill Pond shelf, and sheltered return berth. The hull, deck, and onboard objects share a moving parent. All passengers board by actual planks; shelves, lamps, and tubes have visible mounts.

Before generating a plate, specify foot/prop contact planes, attachment points, walking corridors, and moving-platform parents. Composite the real layers at tablet crops. Mouth-only speech and body-only walking remain the motion style. Plan scenes around actual supporting geometry; do not reuse an attractive river plate by placing Nib on its water.

Choose a story-audio budget from the total target after reserving ordinary response and transition time. Measure an expressive read-through, then the recording and played chapter. Never insert extra practice or hurry speech just to meet a duration. New audio requires its own alignment and cuts. Scripts, art, recordings, and builds remain separate production work.

## Resolution and next-season invitation

The first Storyboat route is complete and can operate again. The keeper of a closed garden asks to borrow an illustrated maintenance guide; that request leads to The Clockwork Garden after this book has ended.

## Writing acceptance

Before a script is recorded, verify that each opening follows the previous end state, each chapter resolves its own question, and each destination follows from something actually observed or read. Check the ledger, review opportunities, independent/support distinctions, and setup/payoff chain. No unprepared word, new magical power, or hidden assessment threshold may appear only at the finale.
