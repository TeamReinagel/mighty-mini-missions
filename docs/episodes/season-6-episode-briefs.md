# The Workshop of Almost: Episode Briefs

Season 6 · 12 individual story plans · October 30, 2026

Read the [season arc](season-6-story-plan.md) first and use the [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) for earlier teaching. These are script-ready planning beats, not finished prose or recorded episodes. All chapters target 13–15 minutes for the whole played experience, conditionally on engagement.

## Shared writing and response conventions

Write narrated connective prose between attributed dialogue. Each outline below gives a setup, complication, meaningful child contribution, and resolved consequence. Expand those beats into a story rather than reading the outline aloud. Keep performance directions out of the spoken manuscript. Speaker names immediately after quotations preserve compatibility with the current attribution parser.

R = review before a hint; M = modeled/guided teaching; A = application before answer-revealing help; S = ungraded story action. A newly demonstrated answer is supported practice, not independent decoding. A word-label choice shows recognition; a tap does not prove oral reading. Speech stays optional and has a touch-supported continuation. Never invent a response the child did not give.

Each chapter has a short review, a model of its focus, and three story applications below. Combine adjacent actions at natural gates when that improves pacing; the five rows are a writing plan, not a compulsory minimum test count. Record first responses only where they change the next moment. Help responds to the actual choice and keeps the next attempt available. Playback cuts after the prompt and before its outcome.

## Episode 1 · Unpack the Almost Machine

**Story question:** Which unfinished invention can become one useful shared station?

**Opening state:** The garden works and its visitor note is readable. Neighbors ask the crew for a portable story-making station that can move between camp, garden, and Storyboat landings. An unused makers’ workshop contains unfinished prototypes, a real hand press, and a door smaller than Nib’s first design. The crew accepts four visible requirements: portable, dry, readable, and usable together.

**New teaching / next step:** un can mean undo/reverse in unpack/unlock; keep the base visible

**Selected review:** pack, lock, the garden guide

**Child-reading targets:** **unpack**, **unlock**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** unpack, unlock, unfold, unfinished, useful, undo

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `planning-3`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. Nib unveils an enormous plan that would not fit through the workshop door. Lark records the actual door width and four user requirements.

2. They unpack a small existing hand press. Lark models un plus pack/lock, relating the parts to a changed action.

3. The child opens the correct storage latch and lays out only the parts needed for one smaller design.

4. The first model is visible but its feed tray faces the wrong way. The next chapter has a specific revision to make.
