# Lark & Nib’s Treasure Quest

## The eight-book story and reading roadmap

Planning edition · October 30, 2026

Season 1 finds a library. The later seasons make reading useful to a widening world: connecting neighbors, sharing stories, understanding instructions, making something, investigating a question, and finally leading an expedition. Each season is a complete mini novel with its own resolved problem. Every episode also has a local question, complication, child contribution, and resolution.

This packet extends the existing Season 1 plan with **seven season arcs and 94 detailed episode briefs**. It plans stories and teaching; it does not represent recordings, art, playable episodes, measured runtimes, or observed learning outcomes as completed.

The later times are **provisional writing allowances**: increase only while the actual child remains comfortably engaged. They include narration, prompts, thinking, gestures, transitions, and the ending. Keep a shorter earlier target whenever needed. These seasons are curriculum/story stages, not a calendar or an assumption that a three-year-old will soon tolerate seventeen-minute episodes. Optional rereading is separate.

The older prototype had 84 later-season placeholders. This packet has 94 chapters because some assumed prerequisites now have explicit bridge stories: separate th and oo pronunciations, additional r-controlled spellings, igh, nk, unstressed vowels, and spelling changes. More chapters do not authorize longer individual sessions or extra drill. Each added chapter has its own complete plot.

## The series ending, worked backward

The final object is a route page another reader can use. It must come from the child’s actual choices, reading, explanation, and observations; a perfect prewritten page is not handed over as a prize. That requires an earlier habit of checking information rather than trusting the most attractive picture. The brook investigation supplies that habit.

The investigation needs records with clear wording, source labels, and an honest distinction between a guess and an observation. The workshop supplies a practical way to make and revise messages, while the garden supplies experience using a guide to understand a real mechanism.

The garden’s request follows an operating Storyboat service. The boat needs the wider community that the post route and lookout signal have helped connect. The post route starts because the library found in Season 1 ought to be useful to neighbors. Thus each season grows from the previous ending without leaving the earlier season unfinished.

The series’s emotional movement is similarly cumulative. Lark progresses from needing a complete map to following the child’s reasoned plan. Nib progresses from announcing splendid ideas to making, checking, and carrying out useful jobs. Both retain humor, curiosity, and competence. Neither becomes a permanently wrong comic foil. The child progresses from joining one little sound to choosing and communicating information that matters to another reader.

## Complete endings, not eight cliffhangers

**Season 2** ends with all three invitations delivered and all three replies returned. A new invitation can be read afterward, but it is not the missing last delivery.

**Season 3** ends with the agreed signal working and acknowledged. The light is an ordinary lantern in its fixed housing; it is not a new supernatural ability or a promise of rescue service.

**Season 4** ends with the four requested books lent and correctly recorded. Borrowers retain their loans for their agreed time. Returning instantly to reclaim every book would contradict the service the story has built.

**Season 5** ends with water, timing, and access verified separately. The garden’s improvement unfolds over story days; reading a word does not make a seed instantly mature.

**Season 6** ends with the portable press fitting the original door and making a usable page from real source material and supplies. The invention meets the requirements stated at the beginning rather than a surprise final test.

**Season 7** ends with a documented local explanation, an ordinary repair, and follow-up observation. It distinguishes what is known from what the notes never measured.

**Season 8** ends with the chosen route completed, the atlas page checked, and the final mini novel bound. Sun/meadow, pine/lookout, and reed/bank branches have different information, equipment, conditions, and endings. The untaken branches remain optional later adventures, not unfinished obligations.

## How the reading builds

Season 2 inherits **m, short a, s, t, p, short i, n, r** from the authored Season 1 plan. It does not restart n/p as new just because an older playable prototype did so. Its opening words already include useful combinations of the learned set; d expands these immediately. Further short vowels and consonants widen the word set before later vowel spellings arrive.

Season 3 completes the planned common basic consonants while teaching selected long-vowel spellings. It distinguishes a letter representing two familiar sounds, as in x, from a single phoneme. It explicitly introduces needed function words such as article **a**, **is**, and pronoun **I** rather than silently changing the sound of an earlier short vowel or consonant.

Season 4 separately models unvoiced/voiced th, moon/book oo, and out/snow ow. Other same-sound spellings can be compared in small supported sets. A sound the child knows does not make every spelling for it already taught. The and to/do have their own explicit introduction points.

Season 5 introduces longer-word strategies and the remaining bridges the examples need. Season 6 then uses meaningful word parts and specific spelling adjustments. A word such as running is not required back when the first intact-base ing forms are modeled. Every new passage is checked against the accumulated ledger, not a vague claim that “all phonics” is finished.

Seasons 7–8 apply that knowledge while retaining decoding review selected from actual responses. They do not add surprise phonics to a cold page. Help may supply an untaught topic word or explicitly teach it, but that supplied response cannot also be reported as independent reading.

### Scope that matters when drafting

The ledger is an authoring spine, not an exhaustive inventory of English spelling or a claim that any chosen book can be read unaided. Alternative patterns not on the spine—such as ea in bread, ie in field, air/are, some ear/eer patterns, ew/ue, and many ough words—must be supplied or explicitly taught before independent use. Common words such as quiet, route, and word are not automatically fair targets merely because their individual letters are familiar. Names and book titles may remain adult-read.

Likewise, a true spoken sound-bank example may have untaught printed spellings. Use it richly in narration, but do not slip it into a required child-reading label. Pair needed capital forms with familiar lowercase forms before expecting a capitalized sentence or name. Actual script passages, not only the example lists, need this check.

## What “same type of plan” includes

Each season file gives the starting state, a concrete ending, full synopsis, backward dependencies, character movement, episode table, object/information ledger, setups/payoffs, teaching handoff, staging, and resolved next-season invitation.

Each episode brief supplies its own question, exact inherited state, new teaching or reading strategy, selected review, limited reading targets, sound/language bank, essential-skill opportunity, four causal scene beats, five planned response rows, feedback, expressive dialogue anchor, physical staging, and end-state handoff. Gates can be combined to improve pacing. These are detailed writing plans; final prose scripts and their recordings are separate work.

Preserve one visible goal, touch the actual object, and keep help available. Record review, supported practice, independent applications, and ungraded story actions separately. A tap is not proof of oral decoding, and finishing a book is not evidence that every introduced skill is learned. No timer, score, sibling comparison, or surprise mastery gate is added.
