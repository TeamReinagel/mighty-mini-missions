# The Storyboat: Episode Briefs

Season 4 · 20 individual story plans · October 30, 2026

Read the [season arc](season-4-story-plan.md) first and use the [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) for earlier teaching. These are script-ready planning beats, not finished prose or recorded episodes. All chapters target 11–13 minutes for the whole played experience, conditionally on engagement.

## Shared writing and response conventions

Write narrated connective prose between attributed dialogue. Each outline below gives a setup, complication, meaningful child contribution, and resolved consequence. Expand those beats into a story rather than reading the outline aloud. Keep performance directions out of the spoken manuscript. Speaker names immediately after quotations preserve compatibility with the current attribution parser.

R = review before a hint; M = modeled/guided teaching; A = application before answer-revealing help; S = ungraded story action. A newly demonstrated answer is supported practice, not independent decoding. A word-label choice shows recognition; a tap does not prove oral reading. Speech stays optional and has a touch-supported continuation. Never invent a response the child did not give.

Each chapter has a short review, a model of its focus, and three story applications below. Combine adjacent actions at natural gates when that improves pacing; the five rows are a writing plan, not a compulsory minimum test count. Record first responses only where they change the next moment. Help responds to the actual choice and keeps the next attempt available. Playback cuts after the prompt and before its outcome.

## Episode 1 · A Seat for a Sea Tale

**Story question:** Where can a borrower sit without blocking the boat’s walkway?

**Opening state:** The library, post circuit, and lookout signal work. A riverside invitation asks the crew to operate a small traveling library boat for four agreed stops. The existing ferry service is not abandoned: a separate broad, moored bookboat is available, with its own caretaker and fixed landing places. Four requested books travel in four dry compartments.

**New teaching / next step:** ea represents long e in sea and seat

**Selected review:** ee in see/tree; silent-e

**Child-reading targets:** **sea**, **seat**, **team**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** sea, seat, team, leaf, stream, read

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `teamwork-1`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. Four book requests and four dry compartments are shown before departure. A spare seat blocks the narrow deck route.

2. Lark models ea with sea and seat; the first requested title is Sea. Nib puts the seat in the most impressive, least useful spot.

3. The child reads seat and helps move it to its marked supported place, leaving the walkway clear.

4. The Sea book is packed for the Commons stop. The remaining books stay in their own compartments, named aloud by the narrator.

**End state / handoff:** Four books are packed, Sea first; the seat is fixed and the walkway clear. The dry chest needs its lid fitted.
