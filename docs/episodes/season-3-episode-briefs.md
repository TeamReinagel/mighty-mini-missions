# The Long Path: Episode Briefs

Season 3 · 14 individual story plans · October 30, 2026

Read the [season arc](season-3-story-plan.md) first and use the [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) for earlier teaching. These are script-ready planning beats, not finished prose or recorded episodes. All chapters target 10–12 minutes for the whole played experience, conditionally on engagement.

## Shared writing and response conventions

Write narrated connective prose between attributed dialogue. Each outline below gives a setup, complication, meaningful child contribution, and resolved consequence. Expand those beats into a story rather than reading the outline aloud. Keep performance directions out of the spoken manuscript. Speaker names immediately after quotations preserve compatibility with the current attribution parser.

R = review before a hint; M = modeled/guided teaching; A = application before answer-revealing help; S = ungraded story action. A newly demonstrated answer is supported practice, not independent decoding. A word-label choice shows recognition; a tap does not prove oral reading. Speech stays optional and has a touch-supported continuation. Never invent a response the child did not give.

Each chapter has a short review, a model of its focus, and three story applications below. Combine adjacent actions at natural gates when that improves pacing; the five rows are a writing plan, not a compulsory minimum test count. Record first responses only where they change the next moment. Help responds to the actual choice and keeps the next attempt available. Playback cuts after the prompt and before its outcome.

## Episode 1 · The Tree Beyond the Trees

**Story question:** Which tree does the hill guide mean?

**Opening state:** The library and woodland post route are working. Lookout Hill’s reply asks the crew to help read and restore an old signal lantern before its next community lighting. The original map remains complete at camp; a borrowed hill guide, protected kit, and courier bag travel with Lark, Nib, and the child.

**New teaching / next step:** ee represents long e; compare with short e

**Selected review:** pen and pin; final consonant clusters

**Child-reading targets:** **see**, **seed**, **feet**, **tree**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** see, seed, tree, green, feet, deep

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `problem-solving-4`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. The borrowed guide names a distinctive tree beyond the near canopy. Nib chooses the first convenient trunk.

2. Lark models ee through see and seed, then helps read tree in the actual direction line.

3. The child follows the written tree clue and checks the forked silhouette from a supported viewing stump.

4. The correct path reaches a supply shelter where the lookout’s glass fittings are waiting.
