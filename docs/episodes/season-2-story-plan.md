# Lark & Nib’s Treasure Quest

## Season 2: Through the Woods

Writing plan · October 30, 2026 · 14 chapters · More single-letter mappings, short vowels, and early connected text

Planned complete-episode target: **9–11 minutes**, including normal child responses. This is a conditional writing allowance, not measured runtime or a reason to extend a tired child’s session. Use the shorter earlier-season target whenever engagement calls for it.

This is a mini-novel plan and backward-design reference. The [episode briefs](season-2-episode-briefs.md) provide the individual scene and interaction plans. The [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) holds cross-season rules and the cumulative teaching ledger. These replace older prototype story placeholders for writing; they do not change the deployed games.

## The starting state

The Treasure Tree room is open; The Map Pieces is bound; the completed map and moonstone are safe at camp. The atlas shows a disused woodland post route. Lark prepares three fresh library invitations, one each for Reed Hut, the Mill, and Fern Lodge. The child brings m/a/s/t/p/i/n/r, not the old prototype’s reset to n.

## Plan the ending first

The crew completes a first reliable woodland post circuit. All three invitations have reached their intended recipients and all three replies return to the new camp post rack. Neighbors arrive to borrow books, and Nib sounds a small ordinary post horn. The route book is updated and their second mini novel joins the library. One reply includes an invitation to Lookout Hill; the postal story is already complete.

## The mini novel

Lark wants the reopened library to belong to the whole woodland, but an old route book is not enough to make its post service work again. Nib volunteers to carry three invitations in a bag rather bigger than his body. The first journey teaches them to distinguish a written clue from an appealing shortcut and to protect the messages while crossing a narrow creek. The first delivery succeeds at Reed Hut. The next leg takes them past a wet route shelter and a tangle of fallen branches to the Mill. A reply has been left where a courier can actually collect it, so delivering and returning are different jobs. The last leg reaches Fern Lodge through sun and mud, using small practical repairs and careful reading rather than a second torn-map hunt. At the end the crew revisits the three stops with a workable rack, clear labels, and a route that matches the actual paths. The treasure is a living exchange of stories, not a bag of letters delivered to people who have been waiting for centuries.

## Work backward from that ending

1. The final circuit needs three completed deliveries and three collected replies: establish and track exactly those six message states.
2. Fern Lodge must be reached with a dry bag and the correct kit: the sun/mud/packing chapters supply those preparations.
3. The Mill reply needs an agreed collection place: show its arrangement when the second invitation is delivered.
4. Reed Hut must be reached by the correct creek crossing: the fin clue, pot marker, and repair kit solve that first leg.
5. Start with fresh invitations written after Season 1, so the new job follows directly from opening the library.

## Character movement

Lark learns that a route is useful only when another person can follow it. Nib learns the difference between arriving quickly and delivering the right thing reliably. The child becomes a courier whose growing words identify places, supplies, and short actions; neither character treats a mistake as a failed person.

## Object, information, and destination ledger

- Outbound messages P1 Reed Hut, P2 Mill, P3 Fern Lodge start in the bag; P1 is delivered in 5, P2 in 9, P3 in 12.
- Replies R1, R2, R3 are collected during 13–14; their availability is established at the corresponding deliveries. No fourth invitation or lost message is invented at the finale.
- The old route book is a reference, never a new pile of map fragments. The Season 1 map remains intact.
- A dry bag cover, kit, short rope, temporary post labels, and route notes travel with the crew; the post horn is received only after the circuit is established.

## Earlier setups and later payoffs

- The overlarge bag in 1 becomes the well-organized courier bag in 14.
- The fin/fan distinction in 2 prepares later reading of whole words rather than first letters alone.
- The three reply arrangements are paid off in the return circuit, not supplied retrospectively.
- The child reads short commands such as Tap it or Pack it only after capital forms and the needed spelling are modeled.

## Teaching and review handoff

Every chapter carries the entire earlier taught set forward. Its listed review is a selection, not a reset. New sound/spelling models precede child use. Words can be read as soon as their mappings are introduced; no later “blending” chapter postpones that work. The cumulative ledger in the series roadmap records the exact introduction points for patterns and explicit function words.

The example lists are deliberately limited child-reading targets. A character name, chapter title, spoken sound-bank word, or narrated instruction is not automatically decodable. Narrator-supplied names and untaught text stay supplied. Capital forms are paired explicitly with familiar lowercase forms before they are required. Actual responses guide support; completion is not mastery.

For each new-code chapter, model the focus and use its sound bank disproportionately in natural prose. For comprehension chapters, state the text strategy and continue selective decoding review. Use expressive speech attributions frequently; audition the intended delivery in the recording. Check the entire drafted child-readable passage against the ledger, not only these examples.

## Physical staging and production

Use the existing woodland path and camp oak with new fixed post-rack layers; add a broad dry creek landing, Reed Hut bank, Mill yard, and Fern Lodge porch. The creek crossing needs real stepping/support surfaces. Guest recipients are shown or described by the narrator; no additional voice casting is required for these plans.

Before generating a plate, specify foot/prop contact planes, attachment points, walking corridors, and moving-platform parents. Composite the real layers at tablet crops. Mouth-only speech and body-only walking remain the motion style. Plan scenes around actual supporting geometry; do not reuse an attractive river plate by placing Nib on its water.

Choose a story-audio budget from the total target after reserving ordinary response and transition time. Measure an expressive read-through, then the recording and played chapter. Never insert extra practice or hurry speech just to meet a duration. New audio requires its own alignment and cuts. Scripts, art, recordings, and builds remain separate production work.

## Resolution and next-season invitation

The post circuit is operating, the messages have all reached their destinations, and the horn and bound book are collected. Lookout Hill’s reply invites a separate later visit; it is not the missing last delivery.

## Writing acceptance

Before a script is recorded, verify that each opening follows the previous end state, each chapter resolves its own question, and each destination follows from something actually observed or read. Check the ledger, review opportunities, independent/support distinctions, and setup/payoff chain. No unprepared word, new magical power, or hidden assessment threshold may appear only at the finale.
