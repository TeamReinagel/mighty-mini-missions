# Through the Woods: Episode Briefs

Season 2 · 14 individual story plans · October 30, 2026

Read the [season arc](season-2-story-plan.md) first and use the [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) for earlier teaching. These are script-ready planning beats, not finished prose or recorded episodes. All chapters target 9–11 minutes for the whole played experience, conditionally on engagement.

## Shared writing and response conventions

Write narrated connective prose between attributed dialogue. Each outline below gives a setup, complication, meaningful child contribution, and resolved consequence. Expand those beats into a story rather than reading the outline aloud. Keep performance directions out of the spoken manuscript. Speaker names immediately after quotations preserve compatibility with the current attribution parser.

R = review before a hint; M = modeled/guided teaching; A = application before answer-revealing help; S = ungraded story action. A newly demonstrated answer is supported practice, not independent decoding. A word-label choice shows recognition; a tap does not prove oral reading. Speech stays optional and has a touch-supported continuation. Never invent a response the child did not give.

Each chapter has a short review, a model of its focus, and three story applications below. Combine adjacent actions at natural gates when that improves pacing; the five rows are a writing plan, not a compulsory minimum test count. Record first responses only where they change the next moment. Help responds to the actual choice and keeps the next attempt available. Playback cuts after the prompt and before its outcome.

## Episode 1 · The Bag at the Door

**Story question:** How can a bulky new courier bag leave camp without snagging its strap?

**Opening state:** The Treasure Tree room is open; The Map Pieces is bound; the completed map and moonstone are safe at camp. The atlas shows a disused woodland post route. Lark prepares three fresh library invitations, one each for Reed Hut, the Mill, and Fern Lodge. The child brings m/a/s/t/p/i/n/r, not the old prototype’s reset to n.

**New teaching / next step:** d as in dip; pair D/d when a capital is needed

**Selected review:** map and mat; final m/t/p

**Child-reading targets:** **pad**, **sad**, **did**, **dim**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** door, delivery, damp, dip, pad, did

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `teamwork-1`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. Three invitations go into three visible pockets. Nib tries to squeeze the full bag through the library door and catches its strap under the latch.

2. Lark models d and helps read pad on the packing shelf. Nib would rather tug harder; the child finds the soft pad that protects the strap.

3. With the latch padded, Lark lifts it while Nib lowers the bag. The child guides the strap through the actual opening instead of making it disappear.

4. Outside, the route book shows a fish-fin waymarker for the first fork. They leave with all three invitations, a usable bag, and one clear next clue.
