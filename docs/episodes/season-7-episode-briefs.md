# The Vanishing Brook: Episode Briefs

Season 7 · 10 individual story plans · October 30, 2026

Read the [season arc](season-7-story-plan.md) first and use the [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) for earlier teaching. These are script-ready planning beats, not finished prose or recorded episodes. All chapters target 14–16 minutes for the whole played experience, conditionally on engagement.

## Shared writing and response conventions

Write narrated connective prose between attributed dialogue. Each outline below gives a setup, complication, meaningful child contribution, and resolved consequence. Expand those beats into a story rather than reading the outline aloud. Keep performance directions out of the spoken manuscript. Speaker names immediately after quotations preserve compatibility with the current attribution parser.

R = review before a hint; M = modeled/guided teaching; A = application before answer-revealing help; S = ungraded story action. A newly demonstrated answer is supported practice, not independent decoding. A word-label choice shows recognition; a tap does not prove oral reading. Speech stays optional and has a touch-supported continuation. Never invent a response the child did not give.

Each chapter has a short review, a model of its focus, and three story applications below. Combine adjacent actions at natural gates when that improves pacing; the five rows are a writing plan, not a compulsory minimum test count. Record first responses only where they change the next moment. Help responds to the actual choice and keeps the next attempt available. Playback cuts after the prompt and before its outcome.

## Episode 1 · The Morning the Brook Went Quiet

**Story question:** What has actually changed beside the garden?

**Opening state:** The story press is working and the garden remains open. One morning the familiar brook beside the garden sounds unusually quiet. The crew brings its field notebook, existing map, small viewing lens, and a borrowed channel diagram. The moonstone remains a fictional ink-revealing object; its behavior is not evidence about real rocks or water.

**New teaching / next step:** write/read a field note that separates observation from explanation

**Selected review:** selected old decoding patterns; note versus story

**Child-reading targets:** **note**, **brook**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** note, notice, quiet, compare, observe, wonder

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `problem-solving-4`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. At the usual path, the brook is quieter and less water is visible. Nib wonders whether it has gone somewhere for a rest.

2. Lark models a field note with place, time, and what was actually seen. The narrator supplies any untaught vocabulary, including quiet.

3. The child records a before/now picture and a short supplied sentence without selecting a cause yet.

4. The notebook holds a genuine question. The borrowed channel diagram shows the next place to inspect.

**Dialogue anchor:** “A brook having a nap?” Nib wondered softly. “A brook we should look at carefully,” Lark replied.

**Physical staging:** Use the same fixed bank frame for before/now views; do not animate water out of an unconnected region.

**End state / handoff:** The quiet-brook observation is recorded with no cause claimed. The channel diagram is ready to guide the next walk.

**Drafting boundary:** Quiet is supplied vocabulary here; qu plus the earlier vowels does not by itself teach its entire pronunciation. It is not an independent decoding target.
