# Lark & Nib’s Treasure Quest

## Season 5: The Clockwork Garden

Writing plan · October 30, 2026 · 16 chapters · Longer words, syllable strategies, compounds, and early endings

Planned complete-episode target: **12–14 minutes**, including normal child responses. This is a conditional writing allowance, not measured runtime or a reason to extend a tired child’s session. Use the shorter earlier-season target whenever engagement calls for it.

This is a mini-novel plan and backward-design reference. The [episode briefs](season-5-episode-briefs.md) provide the individual scene and interaction plans. The [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) holds cross-season rules and the cumulative teaching ledger. These replace older prototype story placeholders for writing; they do not change the deployed games.

## The starting state

The Storyboat has completed its first circuit. A garden keeper borrows an illustrated maintenance guide and asks why the closed garden’s little clock and watering wheel no longer agree. The crew arrives as helpers with a real guide, not as owners of a new magical mechanism. The library, post, lookout, and Storyboat remain operating elsewhere.

## Plan the ending first

The garden reopens with a working water route, readable instructions, a usable public path, and a clock whose chime matches its actual tested cycle. The cause is identified: an obstructed inlet and wrongly reset timing linkage, not a malicious spell. The leaf stamp is collected and The Clockwork Garden is bound. Growth is shown across several days; no seed instantly becomes a mature plant because the child reads a word.

## The mini novel

The garden seems to have one large mysterious fault: it chimes at the wrong moment and waters the wrong bed. The crew first makes the place usable enough to inspect, working from longer labels they cannot solve by looking at their first letters. Shade, a table, clear bank markers, and manageable chunks of print allow the guide to become useful. Middle chapters recover the actual operating sequence from logs, distinguish completed work from work in progress, and compare the functions of the wheel’s parts. The first hypothesis—that the clock itself has forgotten the time—is revised after observations reveal the inlet obstruction and a linkage reset out of order. The final chapters test the repaired sequence and make a clear short guide for visitors. Reopening is a community event, but the earned ending is a garden that works and a record that explains why.

## Work backward from that ending

1. The final reopening needs water, timing, and access checked separately: do not let a ringing bell stand for all three.
2. The linkage reset must follow the actual water cycle; the ed/ing chapters distinguish completed actions from continuing ones.
3. The cause must be observed before it is fixed; earlier shadows, bank notes, and wheel tests supply the evidence.
4. Long labels need strategies before their use: closed/open chunks, silent-e/team chunks, r-controlled chunks, and final-le are introduced in that order.
5. Do not assume legacy prototype lists taught igh, nk, a before ll, or schwa; explicit bridge chapters supply them here.

## Character movement

Lark learns to revise a convincing explanation when the garden shows different evidence. Nib becomes a patient builder who checks what a part does before improving its appearance. The child learns to decode and verify a whole longer word, then use its meaning; chunking is a strategy, not a race or an inflexible rule about every written syllable.

## Object, information, and destination ledger

- The maintenance guide is borrowed and returned with an added copy of the verified visitor note; no older book is silently rewritten.
- Water route, inlet obstruction, linkage, and clock are separate physical states. Inlet cleared in 8; linkage reset tested in 14; final complete cycle checked in 16.
- The picnic/work mat, shade, table, wheel notes, and leaf stamp remain supported objects; the stamp is collected only after reopening.
- The garden’s changes take multiple story days. Reading speeds do not determine plant growth or real-world waiting.

## Earlier setups and later payoffs

- The sunset mismatch at the opening becomes evidence of a reset problem, not a supernatural clue.
- The lamp/shade observations later explain why one early reading was misleading.
- Past and ongoing action notes prepare the operating sequence without turning grammar into a detached quiz.
- The fresh hut page checks transfer before the garden finale; it is not a new decoding requirement at the last gate.

## Teaching and review handoff

Every chapter carries the entire earlier taught set forward. Its listed review is a selection, not a reset. New sound/spelling models precede child use. Words can be read as soon as their mappings are introduced; no later “blending” chapter postpones that work. The cumulative ledger in the series roadmap records the exact introduction points for patterns and explicit function words.

The example lists are deliberately limited child-reading targets. A character name, chapter title, spoken sound-bank word, or narrated instruction is not automatically decodable. Narrator-supplied names and untaught text stay supplied. Capital forms are paired explicitly with familiar lowercase forms before they are required. Actual responses guide support; completion is not mastery.

For each new-code chapter, model the focus and use its sound bank disproportionately in natural prose. For comprehension chapters, state the text strategy and continue selective decoding review. Use expressive speech attributions frequently; audition the intended delivery in the recording. Check the entire drafted child-readable passage against the ledger, not only these examples.

## Physical staging and production

Use a garden entrance, work table, broad water-channel bank, fixed wheel housing, growing beds, and public reading shelter. Water must connect inlet, wheel, and outlet consistently. Place characters on actual dry paths. Clock hands and linkages attach to pivots; a stopped wheel cannot show free-flowing water driving it without explanation.

Before generating a plate, specify foot/prop contact planes, attachment points, walking corridors, and moving-platform parents. Composite the real layers at tablet crops. Mouth-only speech and body-only walking remain the motion style. Plan scenes around actual supporting geometry; do not reuse an attractive river plate by placing Nib on its water.

Choose a story-audio budget from the total target after reserving ordinary response and transition time. Measure an expressive read-through, then the recording and played chapter. Never insert extra practice or hurry speech just to meet a duration. New audio requires its own alignment and cuts. Scripts, art, recordings, and builds remain separate production work.

## Resolution and next-season invitation

The garden operates and its book closes. Neighbors ask how its clear signs and portable tools were made; an unused workshop offers the next season’s distinct project. The garden remains a place to visit, not a machine carried away as treasure.

## Writing acceptance

Before a script is recorded, verify that each opening follows the previous end state, each chapter resolves its own question, and each destination follows from something actually observed or read. Check the ledger, review opportunities, independent/support distinctions, and setup/payoff chain. No unprepared word, new magical power, or hidden assessment threshold may appear only at the finale.
