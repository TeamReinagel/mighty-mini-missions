# The Atlas We Make: Episode Briefs

Season 8 · 8 individual story plans · October 30, 2026

Read the [season arc](season-8-story-plan.md) first and use the [series roadmap](series-roadmap.md) for earlier teaching. These are script-ready planning beats, not finished prose or recorded episodes. All chapters target 15–17 minutes for the whole played experience, conditionally on engagement.

## Shared writing and response conventions

Write narrated connective prose between attributed dialogue. Each outline below gives a setup, complication, meaningful child contribution, and resolved consequence. Expand those beats into a story rather than reading the outline aloud. Keep performance directions out of the spoken manuscript. Speaker names immediately after quotations preserve compatibility with the current attribution parser.

R = review before a hint; M = modeled/guided teaching; A = application before answer-revealing help; S = ungraded story action. A newly demonstrated answer is supported practice, not independent decoding. A word-label choice shows recognition; a tap does not prove oral reading. Speech stays optional and has a touch-supported continuation. Never invent a response the child did not give.

Each chapter has a short review, a model of its focus, and three story applications below. Combine adjacent actions at natural gates when that improves pacing; the five rows are a writing plan, not a compulsory minimum test count. Record first responses only where they change the next moment. Help responds to the actual choice and keeps the next attempt available. Playback cuts after the prompt and before its outcome.

## Episode 1 · Pick Our Path

**Story question:** Which outing does the child want to plan, and why?

**Opening state:** Seven local adventures have ended with useful books and working places. The library atlas has a blank page for one new route selected by the child: the sunny meadow, pine lookout, or reed-bank reading place. The crew has its ordinary kit and portable story press. The child chooses a purpose and leads; Lark and Nib remain capable companions, not disappearing teachers.

**New teaching / next step:** choose a reading purpose and a route; apply known code to meaningful labels

**Selected review:** selected short/long vowel examples from actual needs

**Child-reading targets:** **sun**, **pine**, **reed**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** choose, purpose, route, sun, pine, reed

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `speaking-1`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. The blank atlas page is shown beside three genuine destination invitations. Lark asks what the child would like to see or do.

2. The child reads or recognizes the prepared labels sun/pine/reed and selects one; the narrator supplies any untaught destination name.

3. One short relevant source is opened, and its real first information fills the destination slot.

4. The route is selected, not completed. A second page promises the preparation detail missing from the first.

| 8.1.2 | Demonstrate the stated teaching/strategy with **sun** and a visible worked example; invite a guided try. | M | Keep the same example visible, reduce the step, and re-model rather than marking an echo as independent. |

**Dialogue anchor:** “Where shall we go?” Lark asked curiously. “I shall bring the useful things,” Nib promised earnestly.

**Physical staging:** The atlas draft and three invitations rest on the table; only the selected route becomes the current scene set.

**End state / handoff:** One route and a personal purpose are selected; its destination slot is filled from the first source, while equipment/stop/condition remain open.

**Drafting boundary:** Branch variants: sun route = meadow picnic/flower viewing; pine route = broad lookout sketching; reed route = quiet waterside book stop. Record the actual chosen purpose, not an assumed picnic preference.

## Episode 2 · Another Page, One Better Plan

**Story question:** What preparation does the second source add?

**Opening state:** One route and a personal purpose are selected; its destination slot is filled from the first source, while equipment/stop/condition remain open.

**New teaching / next step:** read a second relevant page and connect it to the chosen purpose

**Selected review:** route label; known instruction words

**Child-reading targets:** **pack**, **kit**, **shade**, **coat**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** page, pack, kit, shade, coat, compare

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `planning-3`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. The first invitation made the outing sound simple. A second page describes equipment suited to the actual destination.

2. The child reads a manageable span and matches its preparation to the chosen purpose, with untaught topic vocabulary supplied.

3. Nib packs only the useful kit rather than the whole workshop; the child explains the selection through words or pictures.

4. The equipment slot is filled. A specific unclear word or instruction can be explored next if help is wanted.

## Episode 3 · Help When We Need It

**Story question:** What kind of help, if any, would make this page useful?

**Opening state:** Destination and equipment slots are filled for the selected route. Any remaining uncertainty is explicitly marked rather than guessed.

**New teaching / next step:** identify a real uncertainty and choose relevant help; help remains optional

**Selected review:** the selected unfamiliar span; known chunk strategies

**Child-reading targets:** **help**, **page**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** help, word, question, meaning, again, continue

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `problem-solving-2`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. The current source contains one marked uncertainty chosen from the child’s actual need, not an invented compulsory failure.

2. Lark offers a sound/chunk model, a meaning explanation, or a replay of the relevant sentence. The child may also continue without help.

3. The chosen support is used on the same passage, then the child applies the information to the existing plan.

4. The unresolved marker is cleared only when the information is understood with whatever support was used. The next task is explaining the plan to the crew.

**Drafting boundary:** Sun uncertainty can concern shade placement; pine a direction phrase; reed a dry-cover instruction. The actual hard word must be supplied or explicitly modeled if its code lies outside the taught ledger.

## Episode 4 · Tell the Crew

**Story question:** Can Lark and Nib understand the plan well enough to take useful jobs?

**Opening state:** The specific uncertainty is addressed or the child has proceeded independently. The plan’s destination and kit remain tied to their sources.

**New teaching / next step:** explain a source-based plan in an understandable order

**Selected review:** selected reading span; route facts already established

**Child-reading targets:** **first**, **then**, **last**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** first, then, last, explain, source, listener

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `speaking-4`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. Lark repeats a slightly mistaken summary of the chosen page. It is a genuine check of understanding, not an attempt to trick the child.

2. The child uses the source to explain destination and kit in a clear order, speaking, arranging pictures, or dictating.

3. Nib takes one appropriate packing job while Lark checks the route source; neither silently corrects the plan without showing why.

4. A short authored summary is saved as the atlas draft. A related second topic will add a useful condition before the outing.

**Drafting boundary:** Sun: where the shade will stand. Pine: where to pause with the sketch board. Reed: how the book stays dry. Preserve the child’s actual explanation rather than substituting a canned sentence.

## Episode 5 · A New Topic on Our Route

**Story question:** What does a related page change about the first cheerful invitation?

**Opening state:** A child-authored summary and two companion jobs are saved. The selected route now needs a condition checked in a related source.

**New teaching / next step:** compare related topics and qualify a first plan

**Selected review:** the original invitation and its added preparation

**Child-reading targets:** **sun**, **rain**, **wind**, **wet**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** compare, condition, if, shade, shelter, choice

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `problem-solving-4`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. A related page adds a condition the invitation did not discuss: available shade, wind exposure, or a wet-bank alternative.

2. The child reads the relevant span and compares it with the original purpose rather than being forced to abandon the chosen route.

3. They choose a sensible alternative stop or preparation within the same outing and record the reason.

4. The condition slot is filled. A longer combined page can now be read to find the actual stopping place.

## Episode 6 · The Longer Useful Page

**Story question:** Can the crew choose its stopping place using the whole page?

**Opening state:** Destination, kit, and a condition/alternative are recorded. The precise stopping place remains to be confirmed from a longer combined page.

**New teaching / next step:** read a longer connected passage in manageable sections; preserve meaning across them

**Selected review:** known chunks and the selected condition

**Child-reading targets:** **shelter**, **platform**, **stop**, **path**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** longer, section, stop, shelter, path, together

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `listening-2`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. The combined route page has two short paragraphs, one about the approach and one about the stop. Nib acts on the first paragraph alone.

2. The child reads in manageable sections, asking for help or a shorter span when needed; duration is not a persistence test.

3. The second paragraph qualifies the first and identifies the chosen route’s actual stop. The child updates the plan accordingly.

4. All four information slots are filled. A final fresh note will check one current condition before the child leads the outing.

**Drafting boundary:** Sun page leads to the shaded edge; pine page to the sheltered viewing platform; reed page to the dry reading platform. Shorten the reading span if needed without changing the child’s topic or concealing support.

## Episode 7 · A New Note for Our Path

**Story question:** Does today’s short note change the plan the crew prepared?

**Opening state:** Destination, equipment, condition/alternative, and stopping place are all recorded from sources. A fresh current-condition note remains unopened.

**New teaching / next step:** fresh reading on the chosen topic and a real plan decision

**Selected review:** taught code needed by the actual fresh span

**Child-reading targets:** **pine**, **reed**, **bank**, **wide**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** new, note, now, path, check, choose

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `problem-solving-2`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. A new note is presented before answer-revealing narration. Its exact child-readable words use the taught ledger; any necessary topic word is supplied beforehand and not tested as independent.

2. The child attempts the note, then chooses whether its information changes the route’s stopping place or preparation.

3. The crew checks the decision against the actual scene after the attempt, recording any support honestly.

4. The plan is ready for an outing even if help was needed. The child leads in the final chapter without an accuracy threshold.

## Episode 8 · The Atlas We Make

**Story question:** Can another reader use the route page the child has made?

**Opening state:** The current-condition note has been attempted and applied with support recorded. The final route plan is ready for the child to lead.

**New teaching / next step:** lead a purposeful outing, verify the page, and share an authored result

**Selected review:** the selected route’s actual labels and instructions

**Child-reading targets:** **map**, **mat**, **page**. All other story vocabulary remains narrated or explicitly supplied until its mappings are introduced.

**Sound / language bank:** lead, choose, check, share, atlas, another

**Essential-skill opportunity:** `teamwork-4`. Practice it through the story action; do not show its name, award a score, or infer it from completion.

### Scene progression

1. The child gives the first direction while Lark follows and Nib carries out the practical job assigned earlier.

2. They reach the selected supported stop, check it against the page, and enjoy the purpose chosen in episode 1.

3. Back at camp, the child approves or corrects the atlas page from the actual outing. The portable press uses its real source sheet, ink, and paper.

4. The atlas entry and eighth book join the library. The explorer badge marks the shared project; the series ends with an accurate page, a finished outing, and freedom to choose another book or route later.

**Dialogue anchor:** “Which way, explorer?” Lark asked with a smile. “Ready with the useful things,” Nib replied proudly.

**Physical staging:** Sun: mat and shade stand on dry clearing ground. Pine: sketch board rests on the level platform. Reed: book rests on a fixed dry ledge. The chosen equipment and conditions remain visible.

**End state / handoff:** The selected outing is complete, its atlas entry checked and printed, eighth book bound, badge collected. Other routes remain optional rereading adventures, not unfinished obligations.

**Drafting boundary:** Sun ending enjoys the chosen picnic/flower-viewing purpose; pine ending completes a lookout sketch; reed ending shares the chosen quiet book. Each ending returns to camp and finishes the same four information slots with different actual content.
